| #  | Mechanic                                                                                                                                                             | Dynamic                                                                                                | Aesthetic                                                                                        | Bartle type  |
| -- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------ |
| M1 | **Cue Power Adjustment** — The player adjusts the cue power before taking a shot.                                                                                    | The player varies the shot power depending on the required distance and desired movement of the balls. | **Challenge** — Players must judge the correct amount of power for each shot.                    | **Achiever** |
| M2 | **Spin Adjustment** — The player can apply different amounts and directions of spin to the cue ball.                                                                 | The player uses spin to control the cue ball after it contacts another ball or a rail.                 | **Challenge** — Players must learn how different spin settings affect the cue ball.              | **Achiever** |
| M3 | **Angle Adjustment** — The player changes the aiming direction of the cue.                                                                                           | The player calculates and adjusts the angle so the object ball travels toward the intended pocket.     | **Challenge** — Successful shots require accurate prediction of angles and trajectories.         | **Achiever** |
| M4 | **Black Ball Outcome** — Potting the black ball at the appropriate stage determines whether the player wins or loses.                                                | The player chooses when to attempt the black ball while considering the risk of losing the game.       | **Challenge** — The player must make a careful decision under a win-or-lose condition.           | **Achiever** |
| M5 | **Cue Ball Potting Foul** — Potting the cue ball results in a foul and gives an advantage to the opponent.                                                           | The player avoids shots that could cause the cue ball to enter a pocket.                               | **Challenge** — Players must balance attacking shots with control of the cue ball.               | **Achiever** |
| M6 | **First Pot Determines Ball Colour** — The first successful object-ball pot determines whether the player plays solids or stripes.                                   | The player adapts their shot selection and strategy according to the group assigned to them.           | **Challenge** — The player must adapt their strategy after the first successful pot.             | **Achiever** |
| M7 | **Rail Contact Requirement** — If no ball is potted after the cue ball contacts the player's object ball, at least one ball must touch a rail or the shot is a foul. | The player plans shots that either pot a ball or deliberately send a ball toward a rail.               | **Challenge** — Players must plan shots carefully to satisfy the rule while maintaining control. | **Achiever** |

**Aesthetic profile:** The game mainly leans on **Challenge**, because players must accurately control power, angle, spin and shot selection to successfully pot balls. **Expression** is also present because players can use different aiming, power and spin techniques to execute shots. **Discovery** is present as players learn how different combinations of power, angles and spin affect the balls.

**Player types**

* **Primary: Achiever** (**Acting × World**), because the main mechanics reward successful execution of measurable in-game goals such as accurate shots, clearing the player's assigned balls and winning the match (M1–M7).
* **Secondary: Killer** (**Acting × Players**), because the game is directly competitive and the player's successful shots, fouls and win/lose conditions affect the opposing player (M4, M5, M6).

**Aesthetic profile:** The game mainly leans on **Challenge**, because players continuously control the character, avoid hazards and attempt to travel farther. **Discovery** is also important because players encounter coins, machine parts, gears and different gameplay equipment. **Expression** is present through the different gadgets and gameplay options that allow players to alter how they approach a run.

**Player types**

* **Primary: Achiever** (**Acting × World**), because players perform actions in the game world to accomplish measurable goals such as collecting coins, surviving longer, increasing their distance and obtaining rewards (M1–M7).
* **Secondary: Explorer** (**Interacting × World**), because players encounter and collect different items, discover machine parts and experiment with different gadgets and gameplay options (M5, M7).
